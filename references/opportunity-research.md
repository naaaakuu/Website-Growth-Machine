# Opportunity Research

Load when deciding whether an audience problem, query cluster, page, tool, or channel deserves investment.

## Evidence protocol

Create a research log with source, access date, market, language, device/location where relevant, sample, method, evidence label, and limitation. Prefer first-party business data for the business and primary/public sources for market context. A sample is not a census.

Investigate promising customer jobs through:

- customer interviews, support/sales themes, and authorized CRM/order evidence;
- actual search results and query language;
- keyword/trend tools where available, labeled ESTIMATED;
- product/service alternatives and willingness-to-pay signals;
- operational ability to deliver the promised value.

## Opportunity map

For each candidate, record:

| Field | Decision use |
| --- | --- |
| Customer job and audience | Relevance and eligibility |
| Market, language, device | Scope and localization |
| Query cluster or demand signal | Discovery opportunity, not a traffic promise |
| Intent and next action | Page/tool/channel choice |
| Business fit and downstream value | Commercial relevance |
| Distinct contribution | Why it deserves to exist |
| Competitor/SERP obstacle | Difficulty and click opportunity |
| Distribution and maintenance | Compounding viability |
| Evidence, confidence, unknowns | Decision integrity |

Stop research when additional observations no longer change the decision. Do not keep gathering data to avoid choosing.

## Minimum decision tests

Before a significant build, answer:

1. Would the experience help the audience without a search engine?
2. Is there a truthful, differentiating contribution?
3. Is the desired outcome observable and operationally feasible?
4. Is the first scope small enough to test?
5. What would invalidate the investment thesis?
