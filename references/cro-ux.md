# CRO and UX

Load for task success, value proposition, navigation, CTA hierarchy, forms, checkout, booking, mobile experience, lead quality, or conversion hypotheses.

## Diagnose before optimizing

Inspect the intended audience and their actual task. Evaluate relevance, intent match, promise clarity, proof, pricing/eligibility transparency, risk, navigation, cognitive load, CTA fit, form/checkout/booking friction, errors, loading states, mobile use, accessibility, speed, and post-submit handoff.

Use a mechanism for every proposed change:

| Observation | Do not assume | Investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price-stage abandonment | Hide price | Value clarity, unexpected fees, fit, proof, affordability, competitor differences |
| Low form completion | Shorten every form | Eligibility, required data, validation, trust, delivery, device errors |
| Low CTA use | Make every element a button | Readiness, offer clarity, page intent, hierarchy, journey defects |
| More nominal leads | Declare a win | Qualification, response, attendance, close rate, refunds, capacity |

## User evidence

Prioritize observed task testing, customer research, support/sales themes, and instrumented journeys. Heatmaps, replays, and AI simulations are contextual observations—not proof of intent or usability—and require privacy-aware handling.

## Intervention design

Make the least invasive change that addresses a verified mechanism. Preserve transparent pricing, eligibility, fees, policies, cancellation, consent, and accessibility. Do not use false urgency, hidden charges, forced consent, obstructive cancellation, or deceptive defaults.

Test normal, invalid, slow, interrupted, duplicate, empty, unavailable, and success states. A success screen must follow confirmation by the relevant system, not a click alone. Check that lead delivery and follow-up capacity work; the website is only one stage of the customer journey.
