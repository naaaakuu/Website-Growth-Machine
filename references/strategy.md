# Strategy

Load for every DISCOVER, BUILD, SCALE, or material IMPROVE decision.

## Decide what deserves optimization

Start with the customer job, offer, operating capacity, and business objective—not a channel metric. State:

- who is eligible and who is not;
- the problem, alternative, value, price/cost, proof, and delivery constraint;
- one primary outcome, denominator, window, system of record, and guardrails;
- what would make a “better” result economically worse: refunds, poor lead quality, complaints, low margin, capacity strain, privacy harm, or accessibility regression.

Use a metric tree: eligible reach → useful visit → task completion → confirmed outcome → fulfilled value → retention/referral. Do not label proxies as outcomes.

## Investment decision

For each candidate opportunity, compare the expected mechanism, distinct customer value, evidence quality, cost to build/maintain, risk, time to learn, distribution feasibility, and a credible no-build alternative. A decision may be BUILD, IMPROVE, CONSOLIDATE, TEST, DEFER, or DECLINE.

Use bounded scenario ranges only when inputs have defensible sources:

~~~
qualified visits × confirmed-outcome rate × contribution per outcome
− incremental acquisition, delivery, and maintenance cost
~~~

Label assumptions. Do not add overlapping keyword estimates as unique people or call the result a forecast.

## Bottleneck review

Choose the bottleneck with the largest evidenced constraint on the primary outcome. Diagnose traffic, intent/offer fit, task friction, lead handling, payment, fulfillment, retention, measurement, and capacity separately. More visits are not the remedy for a broken checkout; more CRO is not the remedy for irrelevant demand.

## Decision record

Write a decision with options considered, evidence, contrary evidence, estimated costs, confidence, owner, approvals, reversible first step, and date to reassess. Reconsider when the offer, market, implementation, or evidence materially changes.
